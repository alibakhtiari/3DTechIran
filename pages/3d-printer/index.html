3D Printer Technologies

Additive Manufacturing & Rapid Prototyping

3D printing is a groundbreaking additive manufacturing technology that allows the physical creation of virtually any 3D object with intricate geometric complexity directly from a digital CAD file. By slicing a 3D digital model into thin horizontal layers, 3D printers build components layer by layer using a wide variety of specialized engineering materials.

The origins of modern 3D printing date back to 1987 with the invention of stereolithography. Initially used primarily for rapid prototyping to validate conceptual designs, 3D printing has matured into a mainstream production methodology used across aerospace, automotive, medical, dental, and consumer goods industries to fabricate functional end-use components, jigs, fixtures, and custom tooling.

Primary 3D Printing Technologies

  • FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling): Extrudes melted thermoplastic filaments (PLA, ABS, PETG, TPU, Nylon, Carbon Fiber) through a precision nozzle to build sturdy, functional prototypes and end-use mechanical components.
  • SLA (Stereolithography): Uses an ultraviolet laser to cure liquid photopolymer resin into solid plastic layer by layer, achieving ultra-high precision, smooth surface finish, and microscopic detail.
  • DLP (Digital Light Processing): Utilizes a high-resolution digital light projector screen to cure an entire layer of photopolymer resin simultaneously, offering incredible speed and crisp detail.
  • SLS (Selective Laser Sintering): Uses a high-power CO2 laser to fuse small particles of polymer powders (such as Nylon PA12) into solid, highly durable structures without requiring support structures.
